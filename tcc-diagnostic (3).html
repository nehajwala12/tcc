Six integrated takeaways

1 · This is a second-stage improvement story, not a greenfield turnaround.

Wynnchurch already ran a people-systems-Multicare-AI program and pruned the footprint from ~523 stores (2018) to 458 at exit, having called the asset "drastically underperforming the rest of the Jiffy Lube network" pre-investment. The diligence question is coverage, adoption and economics of what is installed — not whether to install it. S02 S06 R03

2 · The perimeter is unsettled, and every per-store number depends on it.

Four public counts coexist today — 458, 449, "over 520", "over 500 across 26 states." Benefits, comps and credit ratios cannot be sized until the borrower/guarantor store master reconciles them. S02 S03 S25

3 · Growth must be earned per visit; the market will not hand over car counts.

The car parc is a record 12.8 years old with 289M vehicles in operation, but intervals are lengthening (~5,269-mi average and rising) and JL system vehicle counts fell for a second straight year even as AUV rose. Peers grow through ticket, mix and retention — Valvoline attributes roughly two-thirds of its comp to ticket. S20 S14 R01 R06 R08

4 · Ares now sits on both sides of the system.

Ares credit funds financed Triple JJJ's purchase of TCC (senior secured + preferred) and were joint lead arrangers on Monomoy's ~$1.3B purchase of the franchisor itself, which closed 1 July 2026 alongside the #2 franchisee PVA. That is an information advantage, a governance constraint, and a concentration to manage — all three. S01 S07 R04

5 · The levers interact; a master bridge must prevent double counting.

Marketing, hours, throughput, attach and fleets all draw on the same visit pool; labor "saved" cannot also staff new traffic. One reconciled visit-and-revenue bridge, one benefit register, one covenant definition. Method

6 · EBITDA is not cash, and improvement is not free.

On the illustrative base, full execution earns only +$1.6M in year one after ramp and $0.9M one-time cost; a zero-benefit implementation loses $1.35M. Fleet growth adds receivables; capex and program spend precede benefits. The monitoring system in §12 is built to catch exactly this. Illustrative

Section 03 · Diagnosing the performance gap

Build one reconciled bridge, sort the stores into archetypes, then let the data pick the first lever

Public evidence supports a plausible improvement program; it does not establish that TCC currently underperforms, identify the dominant cause of any shortfall, or quantify available EBITDA. The diagnostic starts with 24–36 months of store and invoice history reconciled to the ledger and bank settlement — a fixed comparable population alongside the full estate, so closures cannot silently flatter averages.

3.1The revenue & profit driver bridge

Revenue = completed visits × average net revenue per visit. Split visits into first-time, returning retail, fleet and other; split ticket into equivalent service price, oil specification and vehicle mix, added services, discounts and refunds. A higher synthetic share can be required specification, not selling success; a larger ticket after losing low-ticket guests can hide falling total contribution. Always show unique customer vehicles, visits per vehicle and retention beside ticket.

3.3Immediate leakage vs structural change

Immediate leakage — verified billing errors, missing rebates, preventable stockouts, wrong hours listings, uncontrolled coupon stacking, unresolved invoice exceptions — can be fixed before any model completes. Record the transactions affected and whether the fix restores revenue, cuts cost or releases cash.

Structural work — retention, manager quality, staffing stability, complex-service capability, portfolio change — needs longer observation and may raise spend first. Do not force a 30-day recognition window onto a six-month customer or training cycle.

What would change the sequencing: healthy retention + long peak queues → throughput before advertising. Spare capacity + declining repeats → trust, CRM and local value. Strong product/labor efficiency → no generic cost targets. Tight liquidity → protect essential maintenance, stage pilots. Structurally unprofitable leases → avoidable-cash exit math (§8).

Section 06 · The growth program

Seven sales levers, each with a mechanism, a pilot and a gate — profitable completed visits, not clicks or coupons

Ranked by evidence, capital intensity and speed. Every lever books into one master visit-and-revenue bridge (§9.5): these are mechanisms for winning or keeping the same visits, not five independent traffic pools. Contribution after all discounts, fees, parts, labor and displaced work is the only pass mark.

6.1Maintenance-appropriate repeat visits — the retention engine

Mechanism. Raise the share of eligible customers returning to a TCC store when service is due — share recovery, not market growth. Start from the installed journeys (coverage, suppression logic, data quality, delivered results); build a customer-and-vehicle view that survives shared households, replacements and cross-store visits; set due windows from observed mileage and vehicle requirements, not a flat 90 days. Separate first-visit conversion, established retention, overdue reactivation, and follow-up on appropriately deferred work — reminders before coupons for the overdue.

Pilot. Randomize eligible vehicles within comparable segments across reminder-only / modest relevant offer / service explanation / holdout, with the campaign calendar visible. 20–30 stores is an operational scope; statistical size comes from baseline variance and the minimum effect worth acting on. Economics. Count incremental completed invoices and contribution net of discounts given to would-have-come-anyway customers — redemption is not incrementality — and track the next cycle to split durable return from pull-forward. Gate. Positive controlled contribution within uncertainty bands; complaints and opt-outs acceptable. Marketing owns journeys; operations owns delivery; finance validates. S33 S34 S35

6.2Net price & discount discipline

Mechanism. Keep more revenue for equivalent work: fewer unnecessary discounts, controlled overrides, no coupon stacking, no billing errors — while protecting deliberate acquisition and recovery offers. Build a net-price waterfall (list → package → promotion → fleet rate → extra oil → filters → fees → refunds → tax) and review exceptions by store, manager, employee, channel and offer; an unusual rate triggers review, not accusation. Compare prices on stable vehicle-and-service baskets — oil grade and spec, quart capacity, filter, inspection, fees, expected time — never a premiumization quota that pushes unneeded product.

Pilot. One element at a time in matched catchments (offer size, eligibility, package framing, or a small equivalent-price move) with control markets and competitor tracking. Decision rule. At an assumed 40% contribution margin and 10% revenue-linked fees, a 1% price rise tolerates ~2.20% one-period volume loss before contribution flattens (curve in §9.4) — an illustrative threshold, not a TCC target; a higher average ticket with lower absolute gross profit fails. 6.3Search-to-invoice conversion — sell scoped certainty

Mechanism. Remove avoidable uncertainty on location, availability, likely price and duration (§5.2 audit). Where TCC and franchisor rules permit, test a clearly scoped starting range before marketing enrollment, then a vehicle-specific estimate stating what inspection could change; persist the estimate into the store handoff and measure quote-to-final variance by reason. Shift media toward catchments with profitable spare capacity and verified conversion; judge branded search separately from non-branded acquisition using geographic holdouts; include agency and creative cost in channel economics. 6.4Capacity, staffing and trading hours

Mechanism. Serve the arrivals already lost inside current hours first — 15-minute observation of arrival → greeting → queue → bay start → completion → payment, tied to skill coverage, breaks, parts delays and outages; medians and upper percentiles; driveaways counted separately. Only then price extended windows: fully loaded staffing, supervision, utilities and security against contribution from visits that would otherwise be lost to TCC — netting movement from other TCC periods and stores, recalculated at catchment level.

6.5Appropriate service completion — attach without pushing

Mechanism. Complete more genuinely needed work per relationship: inspection completeness, explanation, parts availability, authorization. Log per service: eligibility → observed condition → offered → accepted / deferred / unavailable → why; classify needed-now / reasonable-to-plan / not-indicated from vehicle and inspection evidence. A low attach rate can mean young vehicles, missing stock, absent technicians, incomplete inspections, poor explanation or price — each a different fix. Track contribution per visit and per constrained bay minute: an illustrative $150-contribution 90-minute job earns $1.67/bay-min against $3.00 for a $45 quick service — attractive off-peak or in a spare bay, dilutive when it displaces three quick services. Gate. Contribution up after labor, parts, warranty and displaced demand; authorization sound; return rates stable or better; quality wired into advisor coaching and pay. Illustrative

6.6Profitable local fleets

Mechanism. Recurring commercial demand that fits capability and spare capacity — trades, delivery, field service — with a defined lead process (accountable owner, response time, proposal, credit approval, onboarding, renewal review) and every account linked to actual serviced vehicles and invoices. Price each contract after required oil and parts, negotiated discount, program/card fees, labor, approval delays, administration and expected collection cost; set permitted services and authorization limits; schedule into off-peak windows so retail is not displaced. Section 07 · Margin & operating execution

Cut waste, not capability — and read the contracts before touching labor

7.1Labor deployment without losing profitable work

Decompose payroll into wage rates, scheduled vs worked hours, overtime, role mix, benefits, absence, training and turnover replacement — then relate cost to arrivals and completed work. A payroll percentage can improve because prices rose; an added trained person can raise payroll and still lift EBITDA by converting demand and cutting rework. Build schedules from demand curves and required skill coverage; strip opening/closing routines, parts retrieval, payment delays and manager tasks that consume time without serving customers; standardize what strong matched stores do.

Company-specific constraint. The reproduced FDD describes the dispute over TCC's Labor-Per-Operation-Hour metric, the 5 Mar 2025 settlement revising it, and the 6 Aug 2025 consent judgment; the Janesville WI technician unit was certified 24 Mar 2026. Read the executed agreements and map bargaining obligations before any staffing target is set — and never count the same hour as removed payroll and capacity for new work. S09 S45

Pilot measures: total contribution, cars completed, gross profit per paid hour, queue percentiles, driveaways, overtime, rework, required coverage — proven through absences, weekends and peaks, not one well-staffed test week. Store managers get a demand-and-coverage view, not a bare labor-percentage target.

7.2Manager stability and training as economic inputs

TCC's advertised manager remit spans scheduling, recruiting, retention, inventory, satisfaction and store profit; certification bonuses and employee/fleet referrals appear in role postings — evidence of structure, not of turnover levels. Build employee cohorts (role, store, manager, tenure): time-to-certified-proficiency, 30/90-day retention, vacancy days, overtime cover, rework, recruiting cost, controlled for demand and wage market. Pilot predictable schedules + structured onboarding + qualified mentor + visible skill progression + manager coaching; cost replacement from recruiting fees, trainer hours, overtime and the productivity ramp. Incentives balance controllable contribution, appropriate return, quality and staffing stability — serious quality or authorization failures disqualifying; never pay on average ticket, service count or minimum payroll alone. Context: median tech wage $50.6K and a structural shortage (~32K net adds vs 241K openings; entry-level dealership turnover ~67%) make retention a margin lever, not an HR nicety. S38 S39 R09 R10

7.3Product cost — four bridges, never one number

Reconcile opening inventory + receipts + transfers − closing to recorded usage, approved top-offs, waste and adjustments, by specification and store. Keep four distinct bridges: supplier unit price · vehicle/service mix · physical usage variance · rebates and credits. A purchase-price win can hide under a richer vehicle mix; apparent shrink can be timing. Negotiate within approved product and franchise requirements on total landed cost, availability and rebate economics — a cheaper invoice that forfeits a contractual rebate or raises stockouts is a loss, and product substitution to hit a purchasing target is prohibited. Document the addressable base: 2% on everything overstates; changes already in current invoices belong in the baseline. The Pennzoil supply tie (retained by Shell through the Monomoy deal, and linked to the royalty step-up) bounds oil sourcing — bulk-vs-quart economics, pass-through terms, filters and parts consolidation are the open field. 7.4Availability up, inventory down — different lines

Segment items by demand, coverage, lead time, criticality and substitution; set store-specific minimums; use interstore transfers where handling cost is justified; record work not completed for want of stock and test whether carrying the item pays. Cycle-count high-value/high-error categories and fix recurring causes rather than re-booking adjustments. Inventory reduction releases cash once; it raises EBITDA only via avoided write-offs or carrying costs — keep the lines separate, and watch for emergency-purchase rebound.

7.5Quality cost and equipment uptime

Link every incident to store, invoice, work performed and responsible process; separate allegations, substantiated workmanship, planned follow-ups, equipment problems and unrelated failures; capture refunds, repair cost, recoveries, deductibles, employee time and the customer's later behavior. Prioritize prevention by frequency × expected cost × severity; validate reported drops against audits, insurer data and repeat-repair invoices — recurring claims live in operating economics, not addbacks. Same logic for uptime: track bay outages, cause, duration, response and displaced contribution — a modest maintenance spend can beat media or new equipment by restoring a productive bay, and maintenance deferral is not margin.

7.6Support costs and finance processes — build on what runs

Sweep external spend (software, agencies, telecom, payment processing, facilities, waste, professional fees) for post-acquisition duplicates, unused licenses and billing errors; verify cancellation rights and replacement cost before booking savings. For shared services, only cash expense eliminated reduces EBITDA — fewer manual steps and absorbed growth are capacity, recognized elsewhere. Workday (finance/planning/HR, demand forecasting; later maintenance-related AI commentary) and Nominal (>70% reconciliation automation; a four-person workflow moved to agent-supervised, per vendor) are installed capabilities to validate — go-live dates, recurring cost, exceptions, cash avoided — not gaps to re-buy. Point exception reporting at what pays: price overrides, oil usage variances, overdue fleet invoices, equipment downtime, advertised-vs-actual service mismatches — each with an owner and a verifiable financial result. 7.7Occupancy and other fixed expense

Build the property schedule — base rent, escalators, taxes, insurance, CAM, repairs, expiry, options, guarantees, exit rights — reconcile accounting rent to cash rent, and flag decision dates before options lapse or repair obligations vest. Renegotiation creates durable value only where both parties prefer continuity; it is not an assumed pool. Compare renewal, shorter term, relocation, sublease and exit; confirm retained liabilities and franchisor/lease consents. Energy and store expense changes come from actual bills; a reduction already inside closure economics or a purchasing initiative is not counted twice, and deferral is not reduction.

Evidence first: actual requests that could not be completed, customer referrals, technician availability, bay configuration, product availability, local alternatives — and a price at which this store's customers actually want the service. The case deducts displaced quick-lube contribution, recruitment and training, added paid hours, equipment maintenance, insurance, initial stock and ramp losses, plus franchise approvals, permits and landlord requirements.

Illustrative hurdle math. $75,000 equipment + $10,000 working capital → $40,000 annual service contribution; less $15,000 incremental staffing, maintenance and displaced visits = $25,000 pretax cash, simple payback 3.4 years. A 30% shortfall in gross contribution cuts net to $13,000 and stretches payback to ~6.5 years — on ~$85K deployed. Compare against cheaper moves at the same address: repair the idle bay, fix parts fill rate, retain the certified technician. Approval rides on absolute cash return and downside liquidity, not menu appeal. Illustrative

8.3Exit math — avoidable cash, not booked EBITDA

Store EBITDA loss ≠ closure benefit. Some rent, guarantees, support costs and repair obligations survive closing; severance, disposal, remediation, legal and inventory loss consume cash; and some customers migrate to another TCC store, preserving contribution. The incremental closure benefit is:

avoided fixed expense − lost store contribution (before those fixed costs) + contribution recaptured at nearby TCC stores − exit costs and new post-closure obligations

Use transfer rates grounded in geography and prior closures — nearby Jiffy Lube stores are not all TCC-owned, and not all customers migrate. A divestiture separates sale proceeds from operating improvement; a sale-leaseback trades initial proceeds for rent burden and lost flexibility. Neither is recurring EBITDA from better operations.

8.4Capital sequencing by liquidity and evidence

Order of funding: (1) required safety, maintenance and service continuity; (2) proven small interventions with near-term payback; (3) selective capability investment; (4) expansion and acquisitions — only with a repeatable operating model and financing headroom through integration. Keep a live pipeline showing approval, commitment, payment, in-service date, planned and actual benefit: a committed-unpaid project is a future cash obligation. Test whether forecast maintenance capex fits equipment age and observed breakdowns rather than extrapolating a quiet spending period — growth peers are pouring capital into this lane (Take 5 adding 150+ units/yr; FullSpeed past 900 stores), which raises the bar for every marginal TCC site. 9.4Anti-double-counting rules the register must enforce

One visit bridge. CRM, advertising, hours, throughput and fleets are mechanisms competing for the same visits, not five independent traffic pools — reconcile the combined change at customer and catchment level.

Price on the original basket only. Added-service benefit applies to genuinely additional work net of displaced service and discounts; procurement savings only on the documented addressable base, and never again if already embedded in the traffic margin.

Labor = payroll actually avoided. If productivity only enables growth without new hires, the value is already inside the growth contribution. The "other expense" pool must name accounts distinct from labor, product and the program budget.

Expense vs cash vs capex. A one-time cost deducted from year-1 EBITDA is not deducted again in the cash bridge unless payment timing differs; capitalized spend sits in capex, never simultaneously in opex.

Section 10 · Credit & cash-flow implications

EBITDA is not cash, and adjusted is not earned — the lender's ledger

Revenue growth can dilute earnings through discounting, inefficient labor, slower bays, fleet credit losses or claims; an EBITDA increase can still consume cash through maintenance capital, receivables, inventory and implementation spend. The credit file reconciles every initiative from invoice to store profit to consolidated earnings to cash — and holds four earnings definitions apart: store EBITDA, consolidated EBITDA, management-adjusted EBITDA, and the covenant definition in the actual agreement.

10.1Stage benefits; weight only what is realized and sustained

Every benefit moves through six states — proposed → piloted → replicated → implemented → realized → sustained — and credit weight belongs at the end of that chain, net of the cost to sustain. A signed supplier agreement substantiates future rates; actual purchases determine the period benefit. A staffing model is not payroll saved. A four-week run-rate annualized past seasonality and promotion overstates. Keep the permitted pro-forma addback schedule separate from the operating dashboard, and never add back recurring technology, persistent recruiting, ordinary claims or routine maintenance because they are labeled "transformation." Valvoline's own Q3 gap — adjusted EBITDA $10.7M above unadjusted — shows how much definition choice moves the number even at a healthy operator. S14

13.2Quality gates before modeling

Tie invoice revenue, refunds and tender totals to the accounts for sampled periods and stores; hunt duplicated invoices, partial days, system conversions, missing stores and inconsistent tax treatment; reconcile time clocks to payroll and inventory movement to balances. Document definition changes across acquisitions, POS migrations and reporting eras; keep a comp-store eligibility history so stores are not silently added or dropped as performance moves. Customer, household, vehicle, visit and service line are five different units — never interchangeable. Record missingness and match rates by store and period: a retention result built only on easily-matched customers is biased, and an hours analysis without unserved arrivals misses the mechanism. Where data are absent, run a bounded manual observation study and state the limits — never fabricate precision. Meanwhile, start what needs no data room: verified content and billing corrections, missing timestamps, metric definitions, the existing-initiative inventory, pilot designs and contract-dependency review. Hold back the big commitments — savings targets, estate-wide pricing, broad headcount cuts, capital expansion — until data and authority exist.

14.4Method & limitations

Public-source research with a 16 September 2026 cutoff; live pages are frequently undated and may retain older copy; search-index observations are flagged where direct retrieval failed. The six-store website review is a convenience sample — not random, not revenue-weighted, not matched to the borrower store master. No internal TCC financials, transaction data, agreements or Ares position reports were supplied; the report therefore estimates neither actual earnings nor leverage nor attainable savings, and its §9 model is a deliberately hypothetical 100-store convention. No customer contact, employee interviews, store visits, mystery shopping or logged-in system testing was performed; public ownership labels are not reconciled to the legal borrower perimeter. Vendor and management claims remain attributed, not audited. Absence of a public disclosure is never evidence that a capability, problem or contract does not exist. Facts are tagged S##, desk research R##, analytical constructions Illustrative — and the three are never blended in a single number.
